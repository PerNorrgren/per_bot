The paradox at the centre of it
You did everything right.
Your body still won't believe it.
The person who has given the most, stayed the longest, proven safety over and over — becomes the person the nervous system treats as the greatest threat. That isn't a failure of love. It's a prediction your body learned long before this relationship began, and it doesn't update through understanding. It updates through evidence delivered at the level where it actually runs.
The closest person is not the most dangerous. Being held does not mean being harmed.
You're not the only one this happens to
This isn't a book about what's wrong with you.
It's written for the person giving everything and receiving, in hard moments, an anger or a withdrawal that was never really about them. For the person who recognises their own patterns are doing the harm, without cruelty or intention. For the couple losing something they can't name, around the seven-year mark. None of it means the love wasn't real.
