When I feel I have to defend myself — Meeting It Head On

When I feel I have to defend myself — RAGE and FEAR run high. This sits within Complex 4 (Boundary & Fairness), in its over-activated state. This poem implicitly carries all nine somatic / salience steps in order, and works to settle boundary reactivity without collapsing boundaries. It allows firmness without attack, and safety without hardening.

There is space here to stand without bracing.

Nothing is coming toward you right now.

The body is already upright,

supported by what’s beneath it,

steady without locking itself in place.

Awareness settles into where you are,

inside clear edges,

not scanning for intrusion,

not preparing for impact.

Muscles release their tight readiness,

not dropping protection,

just easing the constant hold.

Breath moves freely again,

no longer held at the top,

no longer pressed down,

finding its own depth.

Warmth stays contained,

strong but not sharp,

present without needing to surge outward.

The chest remains firm and open enough,

not armoured,

not exposed,

able to hold ground without pushing back.

The sense of “mine” becomes clear without becoming rigid.

What belongs to you stays with you.

What does not, stays outside.

The body recognises that boundaries do not require force to be real.

There is a feeling of solidity here,

quiet and reliable,

where fairness doesn’t need to be defended

because it is already felt.

This moment does not ask you to confront.

It does not ask you to withdraw.

You remain here,

standing inside your own limits,

able to respond without preparing for battle.

For now, that is enough.
