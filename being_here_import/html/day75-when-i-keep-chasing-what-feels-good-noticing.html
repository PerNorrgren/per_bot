When I keep chasing what feels good — Noticing

When I keep chasing what feels good — SEEKING, PLAY, and CARE intensify. This sits within Complex 9 (Joy–Integration & Exploration), in its over-activated state. This places pleasure-seeking and restlessness in a wider cultural and environmental context, without turning inward, regulating, or dampening joy.

Many environments are designed to keep attention moving. Novelty is abundant. Stimulation is continuous. The next option is always visible.

Enjoyment is often framed as something to optimise. More intensity. More variety. More experiences to collect.

In these conditions, joy can become forward-leaning. Moments are touched briefly before attention moves on. Pleasure is sampled rather than inhabited.

This doesn’t come from lack of appreciation. It reflects systems that reward engagement, speed, and constant availability of something new.

Quiet enjoyment has fewer signals of success. It doesn’t announce itself. It doesn’t accumulate.

Modern life also compresses time. Pauses are filled quickly. Stillness is interrupted. Savouring has little structural support.

Seen this way, chasing what feels good is not simply personal restlessness. It mirrors environments that encourage momentum over settling, and stimulation over integration.

Noticing this doesn’t reduce curiosity. It doesn’t flatten enjoyment.

It simply places the experience within the conditions that shape how pleasure is lived, where staying with what feels good is often less reinforced than moving toward the next thing.
