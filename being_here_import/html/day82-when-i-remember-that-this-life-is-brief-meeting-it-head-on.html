When I remember that this life is brief — Meeting It Head On

When I remember that this life is brief — CARE and PLAY hold steady, quietly alongside an awareness of finitude. This sits within Complex 10 (Mortality & Transcendence), the integrative complex that closes the arc. This poem implicitly carries all nine somatic / salience steps in order, but more quietly and spaciously than earlier complexes. It does not aim to resolve mortality or offer consolation. It supports resting inside aliveness while knowing it is finite.

There is space here to pause without stepping outside of life.

The body is already supported, held by the ground beneath it, part of something older than this moment.

Awareness settles into where you are, not reaching beyond what can be known, not turning away from what is true.

Muscles soften into gravity, not letting go of living, just recognising weight and presence.

Breath moves steadily, arriving and leaving, doing what it has always done, without asking permission.

Warmth stays close, not urgent, not fading, quietly alive.

The chest opens enough to feel being here, and firm enough to stay.

Thoughts of beginning and ending are allowed to pass through without being answered.

The body recognises that being alive does not require certainty, and meaning does not require permanence.

There is a sense of belonging to something wider than this one life — time, matter, movement, continuity.

This moment does not ask you to understand what comes next, or to make peace with everything.

You remain here, inside a living body, aware that this life is brief, and still fully here.

For now, that is enough.
