When I move between giving and pulling back — Noticing

When I move between giving and pulling back — CARE and SEEKING cycle between giving fully and needing distance. This sits within Complex 7 (Contribution & Caregiving), in its oscillating state. This places the push–pull of caregiving in a wider social and systemic context, without turning inward, regulating, or resolving.

Care is rarely requested in balanced ways. Need appears unevenly. Intensity rises suddenly. Recovery is often left undefined.

In many roles, availability is assumed. Those who can give are relied upon. Those who pause are questioned.

Within these conditions, movement between offering and withdrawing becomes a way of staying engaged without becoming depleted.

Giving may feel possible when demands are clear, when appreciation is present, or when limits are respected.

Pulling back may happen when care is taken for granted, when need feels endless, or when rest has been postponed too long.

This oscillation is not indecision. It is adjustment. A system testing where care can land safely.

Modern life intensifies this pattern. Requests arrive continuously. Boundaries are porous. The line between personal and collective responsibility is blurred.

In such environments, moving between giving and pulling back can be the most sustainable response available.

Noticing this doesn’t stabilise care. It doesn’t instruct generosity or prescribe rest.

It simply places the experience within the realities that shape it, where alternating between availability and withdrawal is often how caregiving survives over time.
