When I lose myself in closeness — Reflecting

When I lose myself in closeness — LUST, CARE, and PLAY intensify. This sits within Complex 8 (Intimacy & Fusion), in its over-activated state. This stays close to inner experience, naming merging and loss of self as understandable responses to intimacy, without regulating, cautioning, or pulling away.

There are moments when closeness feels absorbing. Attention moves toward the other person. Boundaries soften. The sense of “me” becomes less distinct.

This can feel warm. Alive. Connected in a way that’s deeply human.

Often, it happens without intention. Care flows outward. Desire follows. Presence begins to organise itself around the connection.

In these moments, being separate can feel unnecessary. Even undesirable. Why hold edges when closeness feels this good?

Later, there may be a faint disorientation. A sense of having drifted. Of preferences becoming unclear. Of yourself feeling slightly out of reach.

This can bring confusion. Was I too open? Did I give too much? Why do I feel less solid now?

But losing yourself in closeness is rarely about lack of strength. It often comes from responsiveness, from sensitivity, from a deep capacity to attune.

Merging can feel safer than staying distinct. It can feel like belonging. Like being held inside something shared.

Recognising this doesn’t pull you back abruptly. It doesn’t warn against intimacy.

It simply allows the experience of dissolving into closeness to be seen as meaningful, shaped by care and longing, rather than a mistake to correct.
