When I want closeness and then don’t — Noticing

When I want closeness and then don’t — PANIC/GRIEF and CARE swing between reaching and pulling back. This sits within Complex 2 (Attachment & Belonging), in its oscillating state. This places the push–pull pattern in a broader relational and social context, without turning inward, regulating, or resolving.

Many relationships are lived across changing conditions.

Time, availability,

emotional capacity,

and expectation rarely stay constant.

Closeness can be welcomed in one moment and feel intrusive in the next,

depending on context rather than intention.

Modern life often intensifies this movement.

Contact is frequent but fragmented.

Connection happens alongside interruption.

Distance can appear suddenly, without explanation.

In such conditions, people learn to adjust continually.

To lean in when it feels possible,

and pull back when it doesn’t.

This back-and-forth is not always a sign of ambivalence.

It can be a response to fluctuating demands,

uneven energy,

and relationships that require ongoing negotiation.

Some environments allow for this movement.

Others reward consistency and clarity,

making oscillation feel like a personal shortcoming.

Seen from a wider view, coming and going is often an adaptation.

A way of staying in relationship without becoming overwhelmed or depleted.

Noticing this doesn’t make the pattern disappear.

It doesn’t turn movement into balance.

It simply places the experience within the realities of how connection is lived —

responsive, shifting,

and shaped by more than one need at a time.
