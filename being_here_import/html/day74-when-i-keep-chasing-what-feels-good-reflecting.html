When I keep chasing what feels good — Reflecting

When I keep chasing what feels good — SEEKING, PLAY, and CARE intensify. This sits within Complex 9 (Joy–Integration & Exploration), in its over-activated state. This stays close to inner experience, naming restlessness and pleasure-seeking without dulling joy or urging restraint.

There are moments when enjoyment feels just ahead. A sense that the next thing will bring more colour, more aliveness, more relief.

Attention moves quickly. One experience leads into another. Pleasure becomes something to pursue rather than something to stay with.

Often, this movement carries genuine vitality. Curiosity. Playfulness. A wish to feel more alive in the world.

But staying with what’s pleasant can be harder than finding something new. As soon as a moment settles, the pull toward the next one appears.

This can feel exciting, and also faintly tiring. Like always leaning forward, even when things are already good.

The mind may frame this as appetite or enthusiasm. Sometimes it is. At other times, it’s a way of keeping joy moving so it doesn’t fade, or so something quieter doesn’t arrive.

Pleasure doesn’t always announce how long it wants to stay. It can feel safer to keep it in motion than to let it land fully.

Recognising this doesn’t slow the seeking. It doesn’t ask you to want less.

It simply allows the experience of chasing what feels good to be seen with more clarity, as an attempt to keep joy alive in a way that sometimes outruns itself.
