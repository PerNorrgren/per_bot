When I swing between defending and giving in — Noticing

When I swing between defending and giving in — RAGE and FEAR cycle between defending hard and giving way. This sits within Complex 4 (Boundary & Fairness), in its oscillating state. This places boundary fluctuation in a wider social and relational context, without turning inward, regulating, or resolving.

Boundaries are not negotiated in neutral spaces.

They are shaped by roles, hierarchies, histories,

and by how much support is available in a given moment.

In some settings, firmness is required to be heard.

In others, it carries risk.

Yielding can preserve safety,

or it can quietly cost something over time.

Many people learn to adjust their boundaries in response to these conditions.

To defend when the ground feels shaky.

To give way when resistance feels too costly.

This movement is often invisible from the outside.

What looks like inconsistency may actually be responsiveness to context.

Power dynamics matter here.

So does timing.

So does whether limits have been respected in the past.

In environments where fairness is unclear or enforcement is uneven,

boundary-setting becomes a live negotiation rather than a fixed rule.

Seen this way, oscillation is not indecision.

It is a strategy — sometimes conscious, often learned —

for staying intact across changing conditions.

Noticing this doesn’t stabilise boundaries. It doesn’t prescribe the “right” response.

It simply places the experience within the realities that shape it,

where moving between defending and yielding

can be a reasonable way to navigate complex and unequal situations.
