When I can’t get started — Reflecting

When I can’t get started — SEEKING runs low and RAGE collapses. This sits within Complex 3 (Agency & Reward), in its under-activated state. This stays close to inner experience, naming stuckness and flatness as meaningful states rather than problems to overcome.

There are times when beginning feels harder than it should.

Not because the task is unclear,

but because the energy to meet it isn’t there.

The day may stretch out ahead,

full of possibilities,

yet none of them seem close enough to touch.

Motivation is often spoken about as something you either have or don’t.

But lived experience is rarely that simple.

Sometimes the body doesn’t respond to intention.

Plans stay theoretical.

Decisions remain suspended.

This can be frustrating,

especially when there’s an expectation

that wanting something should be enough to make it happen.

It’s easy, in these moments,

to turn the pause into a judgement —

to see stillness as laziness,

or hesitation as failure.

Yet not starting is not always avoidance.

It can be a sign of depletion,

of uncertainty,

or of having moved too fast for too long.

There may be a quiet wish underneath it all —

for clarity,

for direction,

for something to feel worth beginning.

Recognising this doesn’t create momentum.

It doesn’t unlock a next step.

It simply allows the experience of not starting to be seen without pressure,

as part of how agency ebbs and flows over time.
