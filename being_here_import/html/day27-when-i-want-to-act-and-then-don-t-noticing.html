When I want to act and then don’t — Noticing

When I want to act and then don’t — SEEKING and RAGE cycle between surging and dropping away. This sits within Complex 3 (Agency & Reward), in its oscillating state. This places fluctuating agency in a wider contextual frame, without turning inward, regulating, or resolving.

Many systems expect steady output. Consistency is often treated as reliability. Variation is less easily accommodated.

Work cycles, social expectations, and productivity norms tend to favour sustained effort over rhythms that rise and fall.

Within these structures, changing momentum can feel out of place. Starting and stopping may be read as inefficiency rather than adaptation.

Yet bodies do not operate on constant drive. Energy responds to sleep, stress, meaning, and to whether effort feels connected to reward.

In environments where outcomes are uncertain or effort is repeatedly disconnected from satisfaction, agency may surge briefly and then withdraw.

Seen this way, oscillation is not confusion. It can be a response to mismatched demands — a system testing whether movement is worth continuing.

Modern life often compresses timelines. Decisions are expected quickly. Follow-through is assumed.

Under these conditions, fluctuating motivation becomes visible, and sometimes problematised.

Noticing this doesn’t smooth the pattern. It doesn’t stabilise energy.

It simply places the experience within the realities that shape agency, where starting and stopping can be signs of ongoing negotiation between effort, meaning, and capacity.
