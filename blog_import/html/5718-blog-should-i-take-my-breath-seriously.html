Why do we underestimate our breath?

Most of us take about 20,000 breaths a day and barely notice a single one. That’s like having an elite personal trainer guiding your health every second of your life—and ignoring them completely. Breath is the most direct way to influence how you feel, think, and function, yet we treat it as an afterthought.

But what if we stopped taking it for granted? What if we tuned in, trained it, and turned it into a superpower? That’s exactly what Attuned Breath Coach - ABC helps us do.

How does breath control change the brain?

Neuroscientists have been fascinated by breath for decades, and research keeps confirming what ancient traditions knew: how you breathe directly impacts your nervous system, brain function, and emotional state.

  • Regulating the autonomic nervous system (ANS) – Fast, shallow breathing activates the sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight), while slow, deep breathing shifts us into the parasympathetic nervous system (rest-and-digest). This means you can manually turn down anxiety and stress just by breathing differently.
  • Enhancing cognitive function – Studies show that nasal breathing (rather than mouth breathing) increases oxygen to the brain, improves memory, and sharpens focus.
  • Synchronising brain waves – Research from Stanford found that controlled breathing creates rhythmic gamma oscillations in the brain, which are linked to higher states of consciousness, deep focus, and even creativity.

So, no, it’s not just “in your head.” A trained breath changes your physiology, cognition, and emotions in real time.

How does breath training affect behaviour?

Imagine two versions of you:

  1. Version A: Rushing, shallow breathing, slight tension in the chest.
  2. Version B: Calm, deep, rhythmic breathing, relaxed shoulders.

These aren’t just different moods. They’re different neurological states, and they determine how you react to life.

  • Social interactions – A regulated breath helps you remain open and responsive rather than reactive or defensive. You listen better, you connect more deeply, and people respond in kind.
  • Decision-making – Breath directly influences the prefrontal cortex, the part of the brain responsible for rational thinking. Slower breaths mean better decisions, reduced impulsivity, and a greater ability to navigate complex situations.
  • Emotional resilience – When breath patterns are chaotic, emotions follow suit. By regulating breath, we create emotional stability, which means we handle setbacks with more ease and less overwhelm.

In short, if you want to be a calmer, sharper, more emotionally balanced person, breath training is one of the simplest and most effective ways to get there.

How can you start using breath to shift your state?

If you’re new to breathwork, keep it simple. You don’t need to memorise complex techniques or sit for hours in meditation. Just start with this 3-step guide:

  1. Breathe through your nose – This alone boosts oxygen efficiency, reduces stress, and enhances focus. Mouth breathing is like driving with the handbrake on—inefficient and exhausting.
  2. Slow it down – Aim for 5 to 6 breaths per minute (about 5 seconds in, 5 seconds out). This rhythm optimises your heart rate, oxygen levels, and brain function.
  3. Use the Attuned Breath Coach timer – Let it guide you so you stay consistent. Just like fitness training, small, daily practice leads to profound changes.

Why does this work so effectively?

Breath isn’t just another “wellness trend.” It’s a fundamental biological process that shapes our health, emotions, and cognition.

Ancient traditions like Pranayama, Qigong, and Zen meditation have harnessed breath for thousands of years. Now, modern science confirms their wisdom:

  • A study in the journal ‘Science’ found that controlled breathing influences the locus coeruleus, a brain region tied to alertness and calmness.
  • Harvard research suggests that slow breathing reduces inflammation, boosts immune function, and even lowers blood pressure.
  • A Yale study on breath training showed significant reductions in anxiety and depression, outperforming some traditional cognitive therapy techniques.

So, whether it’s an ancient yogi, a neuroscientist, or just someone looking to feel better—breath training works for everyone.
