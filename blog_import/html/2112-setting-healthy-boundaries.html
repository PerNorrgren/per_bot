Setting Healthy Boundaries

Originally published 2024-08-21

Personal Growth, Wellbeing and Self-Care

One way to improve our wellbeing and self-care in our life, where we’re constantly bombarded by demands and distractions, is the act of setting boundaries. This can seem almost revolutionary, and yet, establishing healthy boundaries is a powerful way to protect our inner peace and maintain our mental well-being. In a UK context, where politeness often reigns supreme, setting boundaries can sometimes feel like a daunting task. However, it is an essential practice, not just for our own sanity but for the betterment of our relationships and society as a whole.

The Importance of Setting Boundaries

Imagine (as a native Swedish person, I used to find this mildly amusing) you’re at a typical British garden party. The sun is shining, there’s the smell of fresh-cut grass, and someone’s offering you yet another cucumber sandwich, even though you’ve already had three. The polite thing to do is to accept, right? After all, we wouldn’t want to offend anyone. But at what cost? It’s in these seemingly small moments that our ability to set boundaries is tested. Saying “no” to that fourth sandwich might seem trivial, but it’s representative of a much larger issue: our tendency to sacrifice our own needs and comfort for the sake of others.

Setting boundaries is about recognising your limits and being comfortable with them. It’s about understanding that it’s perfectly acceptable to prioritise your well-being, even if it means declining a request or stepping away from a situation. In the spirit of self-respect, wellbeing and self-care, setting boundaries is an act of kindness towards yourself. It’s a way to ensure that you’re not overextending, that you’re not giving more than you can afford to give, whether that’s time, energy, or emotional capacity.

The Brain’s Response to Ignoring Boundaries

But what happens when we fail to set these boundaries? The brain, that magnificent organ, is a bit like a railway system—it works best when it runs on a clear, scheduled track. When we ignore our boundaries, it’s akin to throwing a spanner in the works. Stress levels rise, and the brain’s ability to manage emotions becomes impaired. Over time, this can lead to burnout, anxiety, and a whole host of other mental health issues.

The brain is wired to seek balance. When we continually push ourselves beyond our limits, the brain’s stress response is activated. This involves the release of cortisol, the body’s primary stress hormone. While cortisol is helpful in short bursts—say, if you’re being chased by a particularly aggressive swan in the park—chronic elevation of cortisol levels can be damaging. It leads to increased blood pressure, impaired cognitive function, and even a weakened immune system.

Think of a time when you’ve taken on too much at work because you didn’t want to let your team down. Perhaps you’ve stayed late every night, skipping meals, and sacrificing sleep. At first, you might manage to keep up the pace, but soon, the exhaustion catches up. You become irritable, forgetful, and maybe even resentful. This is your brain’s way of telling you that your boundaries have been crossed, and it’s time to pull back.

Real-Life Examples of Setting Boundaries

Setting boundaries doesn’t have to be a dramatic affair. It can be as simple as a friend of mine, let’s call her Sarah, who realised that her Sunday mornings were sacred. They were the only time she had to herself in a week packed with work and social obligations. So, she made it clear to her friends and family that Sunday mornings were off-limits. At first, people didn’t understand. They’d call or drop by, expecting her to be available as she always had been. But Sarah stood firm. She explained that she needed that time to recharge. Gradually, her friends respected her decision, and Sarah found herself looking forward to her Sunday mornings, knowing they were her time to rest and reflect.

Another example is from a former colleague who, after years of saying “yes” to every project, finally started to push back. He realised that his constant willingness to take on extra work wasn’t winning him any friends or medals; instead, it was wearing him down. By setting boundaries and learning to say “no,” he not only improved his work-life balance but also found that he was more effective in his role. His superiors respected his boundaries, and he became a model for others in the office who were also struggling with over-commitment.

In both of these cases, the act of setting boundaries wasn’t just about self-care; it was about gaining respect—both self-respect and the respect of others. When you set clear boundaries, you send a message that your time, energy, wellbeing and self-care are valuable.

The Humour in Boundary-Setting

Now, setting boundaries can sometimes feel awkward, especially in a culture that values politeness as much as the British do. There’s a certain humour in it as well. For example, saying “no” to a third helping of pudding at a family dinner might be met with gasps of horror from your grandmother. But standing your ground, even in the face of her well-meaning insistence, can become a light-hearted moment that you laugh about later. “Remember when Gran tried to force-feed us all trifle for the third time?” you’ll joke, as everyone chuckles at the memory.

Humour, in fact, can be a great ally when setting boundaries. It lightens the mood and can help diffuse any tension that might arise from asserting yourself. For instance, if a colleague asks you to take on an extra task when you’re already swamped, you might reply with a smile, “I’d love to help, but unless I’ve secretly mastered the art of time travel, I’m not sure I can fit it in!”

Why Boundaries Matter in the Long Run

In the long run, setting boundaries is about much more than just saying “no” to extra pudding or another work project. It’s about fostering a life where your needs are met, where you’re not constantly running on empty, and where your relationships are based on mutual respect. In a world that often demands more and more from us, boundaries are the lines we draw to protect our peace.

Gandhi once said, “You must be the change you wish to see in the world.” In the context of boundaries, this means being an example of self-respect and balance. When we set healthy boundaries, we not only improve our own lives but also encourage others to do the same. It’s a ripple effect that starts with one person saying, “This is what I need,” and inspires others to take similar steps for their wellbeing and self-care.

So, the next time you’re faced with a situation where you feel your boundaries might be crossed, remember the importance of those invisible lines. Honour them with the same dedication you would show a cherished tradition, and watch as your life begins to reflect the balance and peace you’ve created.
