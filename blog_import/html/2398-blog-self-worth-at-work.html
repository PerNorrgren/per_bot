A Guide to Feeling Valued

In the bustling landscape of today’s workplaces, where KPIs, OKRs, and endless Zoom meetings vie for our attention, self-worth can feel like a luxury that doesn’t quite fit on the agenda. Yet, how we feel about ourselves at work can shape not only our performance but also our mental well-being, relationships, and overall experience of life.

Many people—perhaps all of us at some point—struggle with questions about self-worth in professional settings: Am I good enough? Are my ideas valued? Does what I do here even matter? And while it’s easy to pin these feelings on specific job roles or feedback, the roots go deeper into our neural circuitry and behavioural patterns.

In this blog, we’ll explore the neuroscience behind self-worth in the workplace, share real-life insights into how these feelings can transform our behaviours, and equip you with a practical (and digestible) five-step plan to boost self-worth where you work.

The Neuroscience of Self-Worth: Why Our Brains Are Wired to Care

So, why do we care so much about our worth in the workplace? Neuroscientifically speaking, we’re built to seek acceptance and acknowledgment from our social environment—especially in places like work, where the stakes feel high, and recognition is often tied to tangible rewards (promotions, pay raises, those occasional slices of free office cake).

Here are the main brain players involved:
- The Prefrontal Cortex (PFC): The part of our brain responsible for reasoning, self-reflection, and decision-making, the PFC is also highly sensitive to how we perceive others’ evaluations of us. When we feel unworthy, the PFC can get hijacked, affecting our focus, productivity, and creativity.
- The Amygdala: Known as the brain’s “alarm bell,” the amygdala registers emotional responses, including fear and social rejection. If we feel undervalued at work, our amygdala kicks in, often creating an outsized response that heightens anxiety, stress, and avoidance behaviours.
- Dopamine Pathways: Dopamine is our brain’s “reward” chemical, released when we feel appreciated, achieving a task, or getting positive feedback. This chemical motivates us to keep striving. However, if we’re persistently undervalued, these pathways weaken, diminishing our enthusiasm and engagement over time.

In essence, a workplace that fosters self-worth isn’t just a “nice-to-have”—it’s crucial for mental and physical health. And the effects are tangible: higher engagement, increased creativity, and greater overall resilience.

How Low Self-Worth Manifests in the Workplace

To understand how self-worth impacts behaviour, let’s imagine a character, Alex. Alex is diligent, but every time they join a team meeting, they freeze up. They feel invisible and question the value of their contributions.

Scenario 1: Alex’s Presentation

Imagine Alex needs to present an idea. However, feeling low on self-worth, they end up rushing through slides, barely speaking above a murmur, and not pausing for questions—subtly conveying to the room, “This might not be that great.” Alex’s behaviour, driven by self-doubt, shapes the team’s perception of the presentation, making it less memorable.

Scenario 2: Alex and Feedback

Now, imagine Alex receives critical feedback. Instead of viewing this as an opportunity for growth, Alex perceives it as a reflection of their worth. This perception triggers a flood of stress responses, reinforcing a cycle where Alex hesitates to step up and share ideas again.

Scenario 3: Alex Watching Colleagues Succeed

Lastly, Alex sees a colleague get praised for an idea. Self-worth issues can twist this into an inner monologue of comparison, subtly stoking feelings of resentment and insecurity, which, in turn, strain workplace relationships.

These examples illustrate how low self-worth can create a downward spiral of limiting behaviour, even if the initial doubt is unwarranted. But what can we do to change this? Let’s start with a little practical guidance.

A Practical “Do This” Guide to Cultivating Self-Worth at Work

Here’s your bite-sized, no-fluff guide to fostering self-worth in the workplace. No jargon, no complexity—just steps that you can start using today.

  • 1. Separate Self from Work Output. Yes, work matters. But your worth isn’t a reflection of today’s report, presentation, or email response time. Practise viewing your output as part of your role—not your identity. When a task is done, it’s done. Move on.
  • 2. Celebrate Small Wins (Yes, All of Them). It’s easy to forget to celebrate the smaller achievements—like answering an email kindly or simply showing up fully for a meeting. Acknowledging these wins prompts the brain to release dopamine, enhancing motivation and reminding us that small contributions add up.
  • 3. Visualise a Confident Version of Yourself. Before walking into a challenging situation, try this visualisation exercise: imagine the most confident version of you (yes, even if you need to borrow traits from a fictional hero). See yourself handling the situation calmly, with assurance. This practice can trigger a shift in your self-perception and quiet those nagging doubts.
  • 4. Give Colleagues Genuine Praise. When you’re feeling undervalued, it may feel counterintuitive to highlight someone else’s achievements. But offering sincere compliments nurtures a sense of connection and community, reducing that nagging tendency to compare and compete. You’ll feel more valued when you value others.
  • 5. Practise Self-Compassion (Especially in Mistakes). We’re quick to berate ourselves when we make errors, but self-compassion is a proven antidote to self-criticism. When things go wrong, ask yourself, “What would I say to a colleague in this situation?” Extending the same kindness to yourself will go a long way toward boosting resilience.

Real-Life Insights: How Self-Worth Transforms Behaviour

Stories of individuals who’ve embraced self-worth at work show that even small shifts in mindset can produce profound changes. Take Sara, who struggled with speaking up in meetings. After weeks of practising visualisation and celebrating small wins (even “showing up with a good attitude” became a win), she found her voice. Within months, Sara not only contributed more confidently but also received praise for her insights, reinforcing her self-worth in a genuine feedback loop.

Or consider James, who was plagued by perfectionism and hesitant to delegate tasks. By separating his self-worth from his output and gradually allowing himself to let go of minor details, he reduced his stress levels and felt freer to innovate. This shift not only improved his workflow but also strengthened his relationships with his team.

How Self-Worth Affects Workplace Culture

The benefits of self-worth are contagious. In organisations where people feel valued, there’s a ripple effect: engagement soars, collaboration flourishes, and even conflict is handled with more maturity. If you’re a leader, recognising this can transform not only your experience but also the atmosphere around you.

Fostering an environment that reinforces self-worth doesn’t require grand gestures—it’s in the small, consistent actions. Simple acknowledgments like “I appreciate your input” or “That’s a great idea” can change how someone views themselves at work.

Wrapping Up

Cultivating self-worth at work is both an individual journey and a communal one. When we start to see ourselves—and others—as inherently valuable, regardless of today’s achievements or mistakes, the entire workplace benefits. Neuroscience backs up the importance of this approach: when we feel valued, our brain’s reward systems flourish, boosting resilience, engagement, and performance.

So, go ahead, take these steps, practice them regularly, and watch as you navigate work with a renewed sense of self-worth. It’s a small shift in perspective but one that’s proven to enrich not just your work life but your overall well-being.

And remember, next time you’re questioning your value, take a deep breath, visualise your inner “you’ve got this” version, and let the rest fall into place.
